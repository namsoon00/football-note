# Scan Pass Match Model

Scan Pass now opens on a coached attacking-possession lesson. The default surface is one complete, deterministic small-group possession in which learner number 6 receives under pressure, uses the first touch or one-touch intention to shape the next action, connects a teammate, moves again off the ball, and sees the possession resolve as a chance, recycle, turnover, save or block. There is no mode picker, coordinate dragging, kick-power control, timing score, XP award, or separate confirmation button in the default flow.

The lesson keeps the mature football presentation used by the retained match screens: a wide pitch, human uniform silhouettes, a soccer ball, body-facing chevrons, rightward attacking direction, moving defenders, visible possession ring, goalkeeper, offside line, and actual ball/player motion. The default experience is not a quiz card and does not reveal a recommended answer before independent choices.

## Default Possession Lesson Flow

1. **Guided read before practice.** The opening screen spotlights one cue at a time: the pressing or retreating defender, number 8's support, and the future space number 6 can use after passing. The tactical objective is explicit: break first pressure and give the next receiver time to advance.
2. **Coach route demonstration.** Guided mode can play the complete recommended route from the original snapshot. The recommendation is generated by the lesson model from the full continuation, not by immediate pass completion alone.
3. **Independent possession.** The learner retries the same starting situation without a pre-choice answer reveal. Number 4 plays into number 6, the learner chooses the first touch or one-touch layoff, then chooses the next action, then chooses number 6's off-ball support while a teammate has the ball. The learner remains number 6 even when number 8, number 9 or number 4 is carrying the possession.
4. **Continuous resolution.** Automatic teammate and defender actions continue between decisions. Successful branches can reconnect number 6, find a third player and create a final shot; legal inferior branches can keep possession but recycle, or lose the ball naturally when pressure or cover reaches the lane first. The model does not force every branch into a goal.
5. **Full-route comparison.** Review compares the learner's full possession with the recommended full possession from the same initial snapshot. Moment chips inspect receive, touch, link, support and finish. The comparison labels "My possession" and "Recommended route" separately and never mutates the learner record.
6. **Changed-cue challenge.** The final loop switches to a paired situation with the same unrelated attacking start but a meaningful defender cue change. The first defender retreats into cover instead of pressing, so the model recommendation changes from touch-away support play to forward turn, release and reconnection. Multiple routes can be useful; the coach shows one recommendation, not a unique football answer.

Poor but legal choices still execute. They may keep possession under pressure, lose a passing lane, expose back-space, or be intercepted/offside where the authored situation makes that consequence relevant. The trainer never silently normalizes every branch to success and never auto-selects a best route.

## Supplementary Judgment Practice Catalog

The previous 48-scene judgment episode trainer is retained as supplementary practice from the Scan Pass practice menu. It is no longer the default entry.

`JudgmentEngine.catalog()` contains 48 base scenes: eight families with six authored configurations each. The six configurations are organized as three cue pairs. A pair keeps the family and broad local picture recognizable, but changes the meaningful motion cue: a checking run becomes a run behind, an overlap becomes an underlap, a lane defender steps instead of screens, or a recovering defender covers the back-space instead of staying flat. Mirroring and small seeded shifts may vary screen placement, but the configurations are different geometry and movement conditions, not jittered duplicates.

| Family | Six configuration themes |
| --- | --- |
| Rear pressure | close rear presser, dropping presser, curved rear press, checking support, runner behind, curved press with cover |
| Side pressure | pressure from upper side, pressure from lower side, side trap recovering inside, overlap, underlap, inside recovery |
| Open front / second defender | open carry lane, second defender stepping, lane screened while front appears open, check-to-ball, wide release, cover defender recovering |
| Passing-lane defender | defender commits to the ball, defender screens forward lane, defender jumps the return lane, lane defender stepping, lane defender screening, transition recovery into the lane |
| Receiver support / pressure | third-player support near the receiver, isolated receiver, wide underlap support, checking support, late support, third-player run |
| Numerical advantage | central overload, wide 2v1, spare player behind pressure, recovering cover, underlap spare player, back-space reset |
| Post-pass support angle | inside return angle, flat covered angle, blind-side/wide support angle, checked return angle, wide return angle, tracking defender |
| Transition / back-space | secure reset under counter-pressure, back-space behind the ball, high-line/offside forward option, recovery pressure, checked forward option, forward runner returning onside |

A ten-scene session mixes families and configurations. The first eight scheduled slots cover all eight families unless the learner explicitly replaces a next slot with cue-pair practice. The final two slots are delayed cue-pair practice by default and can also be replaced by a missed family when the learner loses possession or exposes a key cue. Retry replaces the record for the same scene instead of adding an extra completed scene, and cue-pair practice consumes the next existing slot rather than extending the session.

## Causal Rules

The default possession lesson and the supplementary judgment trainer are deterministic. Player positions, velocities, simultaneous ball/defender movement, nearest defensive pressure, offside line and predicted next outlets determine the qualitative outcome. Multiple teammates move with authored attacking intentions during observation, and multiple defenders press, cover lanes, track relevant runs or recover behind the ball during observation and execution. There is no preferred-action answer key: changing a family label without changing the geometry cannot change the assessment. Body orientation is drawn to explain motion; technical execution is automatic. Transitions use the same `AttackState` and `AttackTransition` infrastructure as the continuous attack view. The ball travels along the actual selected branch; defenders do not teleport between decisions.

The default `ScanPassPossessionLessonEngine` uses one small-group lesson with a paired changed-cue variant. It enumerates complete candidate continuations, preferring an actual shooting opportunity, retained possession and useful receiver/support connections. First-touch progress breaks otherwise equal recommendations; the model does not reward goal scoring or elapsed time in the UI. The recommended branch uses exactly the same transition functions as the learner. Relabeling an identical scene leaves every outcome and recommendation unchanged.

Each pass leads the receiver and samples 80 simultaneous ball/defender positions. Offside is checked against the ball and second-last opponent at the kick; an earlier interception still takes precedence. Carries and support runs also sample defensive contact. During a support run, the ball follows its current carrier while learner number 6 moves to the chosen space. A teammate can reject a blocked return and recycle; a completed return continues through a forward pass, optional carry and a real shot trajectory. Shot interception by an outfield defender or goalkeeper produces a block or save; an unobstructed shot reaches the goal. These are deterministic, normalized 2D teaching approximations: the model does not simulate ball height, physical contact, elite player speeds, tactical team AI or probabilities.

The two default coach demonstrations last approximately 15–16 seconds at normal animation settings. Observation and movement can be paused manually. Help, app backgrounding and opening another practice route suspend playback. Reduced-motion settings jump to stable states. The pitch reserves the same control region across phases, keeping spatial framing stable; small landscape layouts use a side dock. Review only offers moments that actually occurred, hides later path trails while inspecting an earlier moment, and derives numbered pass options from that moment's state. Whole-route comparison includes the coach's later choices as well as the initial decision; it is not presented as an isolated first-touch experiment.

The following short-episode details apply to the retained supplementary judgment trainer:

First-touch actions move number 6, shield briefly, or return the ball. Number 6 is not moved by background AI during learner-controlled support decisions; if number 6 is the actual moving pass target, the pass leads the moving receiver instead of snapping to a stale position. Next-action decisions evaluate pass interception/offside, forward carry pressure, and whether protecting possession leaves another action available. Support decisions move number 6 off the ball while the teammate holds possession, check pressure throughout that movement, and execute an actual return pass. The ball can be lost while support arrives or intercepted on the return. Different first touches can therefore leave different viable support routes later in the same episode.

First-touch cue arrows refer to the observed incoming pass, not movement that happened only after the learner chose. Coaching details name the current ball carrier, nearest opponent, and numbered reachable connections before and after the choice. Same-snapshot advice checks player velocity and body direction as well as position.

The coaching text is evidence-based. It is built from the executed transition and same-snapshot alternatives, naming the observed actor number and whether the cue was pressure, cover, receiver movement, support movement, recovery or offside positioning. A completed pass is not automatically treated as useful support; the review distinguishes the pass arriving from the receiver still having a playable next connection. The copy describes a repeatable reading habit and does not claim that a real coach assessed the learner.

The design is guided by two coaching principles from FIFA Training Centre material: receiving should use scanning, body shape and first touch to create the next lane or return angle, and defending should combine closest pressure with covering defenders who adapt to the ball and pass. These references guide the instructional model; they are not evidence of measured training transfer. References: [FIFA Passing and receiving](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/in-possession/john-peacock-passing-and-receiving.php), [FIFA Defending as a unit](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/out-of-possession/defending-as-a-unit.php).

Default lesson outcomes distinguish a chance (with actual shot result), recycled possession and turnover. Supplementary episode outcomes are space gained, possession retained, next options preserved, or possession lost. The UI does not award XP, score speed, infer gaze, claim measured scanning skill, or promise real-world transfer.

## Controls and Accessibility

The pitch remains responsive across desktop, phone, small phone, and landscape layouts. The header/status strip and action controls are compact so the pitch keeps the largest usable area. The default possession lesson keeps one stable full-route frame that includes all decision-relevant defenders, receivers, support spaces, the goal and attacking direction, and applies the same transform to field lines, players, ball, targets, cue graphics and hit boxes. Pitch targets remain distinct 48px semantic buttons. Targets use separated callouts with neutral guide lines, avoiding overlap with each other and player silhouettes on small screens. The pitch itself is rendered as an image; raw coordinate gesture handlers are not used for the default lesson choices, avoiding synthesized center-tap semantics.

Keyboard and screen-reader users receive the same decision set through the dock buttons. Replay, retry, compare alternative, continue, free attack and help are all reachable without scrolling excessively in the tested desktop and phone layouts.

## Retained Legacy Surfaces

The previous continuous attack remains available from the Scan Pass practice menu as **Free attack**. That mode still uses `AttackEngine`, keeps one possession moving across the same pitch, and retains the goal, keeper, offside, undo, and full replay behavior from the earlier version.

`JudgmentEpisodeTrainingScreen` remains available from the Scan Pass practice menu as **48-scene judgment practice**. It still provides the broad catalog of shorter cue-pair episodes, but it is not the default Scan Pass entry.

`TouchChainTrainingScreen` remains available for regression coverage and optional legacy use. It still models the longer first-touch chain in which the learner controls the same number 6 through first touch, next action, and off-ball return movement with explicit commit controls. It is no longer the default Scan Pass entry.

The earlier `SpaceTrainingScreen` and its engine remain for regression coverage but are no longer the default entry. Its receive-position/timing exercise is not part of the new possession lesson.

## Earlier scenario lesson implementation

The earlier decision-learning widget is retained for regression coverage; it is no longer the default route. Its opening lesson starts with #4 passing into learner #6 while teammates and defenders move, then asks the learner to choose the next action after reading the cue before reception. The available decisions are forward pass to #8, wide pass to #7, return pass to #4, and forward carry.

The trainer uses three lesson families: rear pressure, passing lane, and receiver support. Each guided set contains six paired situations: the original cue and the changed cue for each family. The changed scene keeps unrelated actors in the same opening positions so the learner can compare cause and consequence rather than memorize one route.

Preview selection is intentionally neutral. Tapping a player or carry space draws an intended dotted route and enables execute, but it does not call assessment logic or reveal safe/unsafe verdicts. A choice and explicit execution can be prepared during the incoming pass. The UI calls `DecisionEngine.assess()` at reception or the later committed simulation time. It plays the returned action, first touch and possible follow-up, then freezes the receiving moment for a causal review. The qualitative outcome, one reason, pressure ring and next-outlet arrows describe that same receiving state. A completed pass can still leave a receiver isolated; a pressured receiver can be a useful wall player when a short, reachable outlet exists.

Reading modes:

- Read together: self-paced reception freeze with simple cue arrows.
- Read alone: self-paced scenes without cue arrows. Independent practice sets shuffle the six scene variants, so the round number does not reveal a fixed answer sequence.
- Moving pressure: after reception, the scenario evolves in active foreground simulation ticks via `decisionState(elapsed)`. Help, backgrounding and opening Free attack pause it; mode changes cancel the previous scene. The short practice window pauses after 2.8 simulation seconds if nothing is executed. This is an ungraded opportunity to watch again, not proof that every pass has become impossible. A selected but unexecuted action is distinguished from no selection. There is no automatic pass, countdown score or speed bonus.

Review comparisons animate from the original scenario and exactly the original decision delay. Viewing another choice does not replace or mutate the stored learner assessment or add a new learning record. A branch label remains visible throughout playback and review. The paths drawn during animation never run ahead of the current ball; the receiving freeze shows the learner action and reachable next outlets, rather than future completed passes.

The decision model samples moving defenders against the ball path. It checks pressure both at arrival and shortly afterwards, allows more time to turn for a receiver facing backwards, and restricts pressured one-touch outlets by distance, body direction, interception and the next receiver's space. Several choices may be useful. A carry can draw or evade a lane defender, while a return pass may keep possession. These are transparent teaching assumptions, not measured elite-player thresholds or validated probabilities.

Regression examples cover all mirrored/translated variants: carrying into rear pressure loses the ball while carrying with the same defender dropping away creates progression; the same forward pass is intercepted when the lane is covered and connects when the defender steps away; the same completed pass into a marked #8 creates a connection through nearby #10 but leaves #8 isolated when #10 moves out of immediate supporting range. Swapping scene labels without changing geometry does not change the assessment.

The previous continuous attack remains available from the Scan Pass practice menu as Free attack. That mode still uses `AttackEngine`, keeps one possession moving across the same pitch, and retains the goal, keeper, offside, undo, and full replay behavior from the earlier version.

The current Scan Pass flows do not award XP, numeric skill scores or persistent challenge history. The possession lesson review compares the current learner route and coach route in memory only; the supplementary judgment trainer's end-of-set review is likewise local to the screen. The screen is an instructional simulator, not a validated measurement of gaze, scanning habit, match skill, or improvement.

The main learning surface remains the pitch. Players use the same human uniform silhouette; #6 is the learner and the possession ring follows the actual ball carrier. A small chevron beside each outfield player shows body orientation. Defenders use warm colors, teammates use blue, the ball is rendered as a soccer ball, and the right-side goal and keeper stay visible. Observation, committed actions and comparison replays each supply the painter with the active transition, so the ball travels along its actual flight path instead of attaching to the passer during flight. A raster regression checks the white ball and black panels at the interpolated position for incoming and outgoing passes. The same world frame is used through observation, choice, execution, and review. The viewport fits the space available above each phase’s controls; the overview toggle restores the fitted full-pitch aspect. Coordinates are a schematic tactical view, not a metrically scaled full-match broadcast.

The domain is an instructional approximation, not a full match model. Coordinates represent player positions, not body-part tracking. Player motion, pressure, passing lanes, receiver follow-up and goalkeeper behavior are deterministic teaching approximations. The retained free-attack offside explanation follows IFAB Law 11: offside is judged at the moment the ball is played, with the ball, halfway line, and second-last opponent including the goalkeeper all relevant. Throw-ins, corners, fouls, restarts and interference-with-an-opponent cases are not simulated. Rule reference: [IFAB Law 11](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/offside/).
